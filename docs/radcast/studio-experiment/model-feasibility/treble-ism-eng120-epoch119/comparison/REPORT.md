These are alignment checks, not clean-reference quality scores or evidence of Adobe's mechanism.

Each group uses only downward static speech-RMS matching to its quietest member and an identical extra sample-peak reduction if needed to stay below −3 dBFS. These controls preserve dynamics within each excerpt and expose differences in speech/room balance; they are not newly processed masters.

Both candidates retain zero measured lag/drift/clipped samples. The slight differences in preservation, pauses and envelope shape do not establish improved room dryness. In the target phrase, the Adobe energy share remains higher than the new candidate by 4.43 dB in 1.5–4 kHz and 5.03 dB in 4–8 kHz; above 8 kHz Adobe has less energy share. These describe spectral distribution, not an inferred EQ or Adobe model architecture, and should not be converted into a quality target by themselves.

The rejection began at the raw model stage: worst protected voiced-window gain was −27.81 dB relative to its median. Highpass alone had no such loss (worst −0.20 dB). Presence/pause control did not account for the loss; leveling rescued some contexts but cannot recover content removed by the model. Final unblended worst relative gain was −23.08 dB, versus −5.08 dB after the single repair. Affected original regions were extremely faint, often −54 to −59 dBFS overall 1 s RMS. The mask is an acoustic periodicity/modulation proxy, not semantic proof. Context-limited cached ASR on the original emitted tentative leading/trailing speech, consistent with some prior B ASR, but the words vary by context and can be hallucinated. Human identification of quiet articulation remains necessary. The 20% blend also returns original room/noise.
